## PART 2 — DEEP EXPLAINER (Beginner-Friendly with Buzzwords)

> This section explains PHANTOM in simple words so you can answer any follow-up naturally and confidently.

---

### What problem are we actually solving?

Banks have thousands of employees. These employees have access to customer accounts, transaction systems, and sensitive financial data as part of their daily job. The problem is — **what happens when one of these trusted employees decides to misuse that access?**

Unlike a hacker who has to break in from outside, an insider already has the keys. They don't need to bypass any firewall or crack any password. They just open the door they're already allowed to open. This makes them essentially **invisible to traditional security systems** — because those systems are designed to stop people who shouldn't be there, not people who are already authorized.

This is called **Privileged Access Misuse** — and it's the hardest type of fraud to catch. Every major Indian banking scandal in recent years — New India Co-op Bank, IndusInd, Punjab & Maharashtra — involved insiders using legitimate access. The GM of New India Co-op Bank stole ₹122 crores over 5 years and no automated system flagged it.

---

### Why does existing software fail?

Current tools — called UEBA (User and Entity Behavior Analytics) — work by watching what employees **do** and flagging things that look unusual. Did someone suddenly download 10,000 records? Flag it. Did someone log in at 3am? Flag it.

But here's the problem: a smart insider knows this. So they do the fraud slowly. A little bit every day. During normal hours. In a way that looks completely routine. The behavior never becomes unusual enough to trigger an alert.

There's also a deeper gap: **these tools only look at what you're doing. They never look at what you're NOT doing.** And this is where PHANTOM is genuinely different.

---

### The Core Insight: The Negative Access Signal

Think about it this way. Imagine a bank teller who normally does 50 different things every day — checks balances, files reports, processes overrides, reconciles accounts. If that teller suddenly starts doing only 20 of those things, and specifically stops touching the functions that create audit logs and compliance trails — that's suspicious. It looks like someone who knows the audit system and is deliberately avoiding it.

PHANTOM calls this the **Negative Access Signal** — the pattern of what an employee is strategically **not** doing. We build a statistical model of what every employee normally accesses based on their role and peer group (this is called a **Peer Cohort Access Distribution**). Then we track the gap — called the **Access Void Score** — between what they should be doing and what they're actually doing. A growing gap, especially in audit-sensitive functions, is a red flag.

This is our primary innovation and no existing product does this. It's built using a machine learning technique called **Isolation Forest**, which is specially designed to detect outliers and anomalies.

---

### The Four Engines — What Each One Does

**Sachin built Engines 1 and 3.**

**Engine 1 — Temporal Causal Chain (Sachin's)**

Think of every action a bank employee takes as a dot. Engine 1 connects those dots into a graph — and then looks for patterns in that graph that match known pre-fraud sequences. Fraudsters don't just randomly steal data. They follow a playbook: first they look around (reconnaissance), then they identify their target, then they extract. Engine 1 learns what that playbook looks like as a sequence of access events, and can detect it **2 to 5 days before any data actually leaves the bank**. It uses a type of AI called a **Graph Transformer** — the same family of models that power modern language AI, but applied to network graphs instead of text.

**Engine 3 — Multi-Party Collusion Graph (Sachin's)**

Most frauds in Indian banking don't involve just one person. There's usually a group — a teller, a manager, maybe someone in IT — all coordinating together. The problem is, if you look at each person individually, none of them looks suspicious. Engine 3 builds a network graph of all employees and tracks when multiple people are accessing the same resources in a coordinated pattern. It uses a **Heterogeneous Attention Network (HAN)** — a type of Graph Neural Network — to score the entire cluster, not just individuals. This is the only engine in any proposed solution that reasons at the **group level**.

---

**Vishal built Engines 2 and 4.**

**Engine 2 — Negative Access Profiler (Vishal's)**

This is the novel one — described above. Vishal built the Peer Cohort Distribution model and the Isolation Forest anomaly scorer. He also built the entire data pipeline — the **Apache Kafka** ingestion system that collects telemetry from Finacle, IAM, LDAP, and PAM sessions in real time, and the **Apache Flink** streaming processor that computes features on 30-second rolling windows.

**Engine 4 — Justification NLP Scorer (Vishal's)**

Every time a bank employee does something unusual — bypasses a standard check, overrides an approval requirement — they have to write a reason in a text field. Most systems ignore this text completely. Vishal's Engine 4 reads it using **IndicBERT**, an AI language model trained on Indian languages including Hindi and Hinglish. It scores each justification on four dimensions:
- **Semantic vagueness** — is it suspiciously generic? ("Customer requested, senior approved")
- **Template reuse** — is the employee copying and pasting the same excuse every time?
- **Urgency injection** — are they using artificial urgency to bypass controls? ("Emergency, can't wait")
- **Social engineering markers** — are they invoking authority they don't have?

A legitimate justification and a corrupt one have measurably different language patterns. IndicBERT can tell the difference.

---

**Shresth built the DITS Engine, SOC Dashboard, and Integration Layer.**

**The DITS Engine (Shresth's)**

DITS stands for Dynamic Identity Trust Score. Think of it as a credit score for employee trustworthiness — but updated every 30 seconds and based on live behavior, not history. Shresth built the **Bayesian Network** that takes the four engine scores (0–100 each) and fuses them into one final score. The reason it's a Bayesian network and not just an average is that the four engines are not independent — a high collusion score should make us more suspicious about access avoidance, and the Bayesian model captures these conditional relationships correctly.

The score triggers three levels of response:
- **0–39 (Trusted):** Normal access, passive monitoring, zero friction for the employee
- **40–69 (Elevated):** Silent alert to the Security Operations Center (SOC), dual-approval required for high-value actions, extended session recording
- **70–100 (Critical):** Instant session freeze, escalation to human supervisor, automated generation of an RBI-compliant evidence package

**The SOC Dashboard (Shresth's)**

Shresth built the live dashboard that security analysts use to monitor all of this. It shows DITS scores updating in real time for every employee, a D3.js force-directed graph showing the collusion network, trend charts showing how scores have evolved over 14 days, and a one-click evidence package generator for any case that needs to be escalated to legal or RBI audit.

**The Integration Layer (Shresth's)**

Shresth built the connectors that make PHANTOM plug into existing bank infrastructure without touching the core banking system. It connects to Finacle/CBS via Kafka webhooks, to IAM and LDAP via the SCIM 2.0 standard, and to existing SIEM tools like Splunk and IBM QRadar. It is compliant with the **DPDP Act 2023** (India's data protection law) — raw telemetry is never stored, only processed into anonymized feature vectors, and all ML inference runs on-premises inside the bank's own servers.

## PART 3 — JUDGE Q&A PREPARATION

### 🔴 HIGH PROBABILITY — Almost certain to be asked

---

**Q1: What makes PHANTOM different from existing UEBA tools like Securonix, Exabeam, or IBM QRadar?**

> **A:** Existing UEBA tools only analyze what employees are doing and flag statistical deviations from their baseline behavior. PHANTOM introduces two things they don't have. First, the Negative Access Profiler — no UEBA product models what employees strategically avoid. Second, group-level collusion detection — existing tools analyze individuals in isolation and miss coordinated multi-employee fraud, which is the dominant pattern in Indian banking cases. Additionally, we have India-specific NLP with IndicBERT for Hindi and Hinglish audit notes, which English-only models completely miss.

---

**Q2: What is the Negative Access Profiler exactly? How does it work?**

> **A:** For each employee, we build a statistical distribution of all the banking system functions that employees with their role, branch, and tenure typically access in a week — we call this the Peer Cohort Access Distribution. We then compute an Access Void Score — how many expected functions is this employee not accessing, and specifically, are the avoided functions the ones that generate audit trails? A bank employee planning fraud will systematically avoid functions that log their activity. This contraction of behavior before a fraud event is documented in every post-incident forensic report we studied. We model it using Isolation Forest, an anomaly detection algorithm, tuned to distinguish between normal access variation and strategic avoidance.

---

**Q3: How do you handle false positives? Won't this flag innocent employees?**

> **A:** Our three-tier intervention model is specifically designed to minimize friction. Scores 0–39 trigger zero intervention — the employee experiences nothing. Scores 40–69 trigger silent background alerts only — the employee still works normally. Only scores 70 and above trigger any visible response, and even then it requires human supervisor confirmation before any action is taken. Our target false-positive rate is below 0.3%. The Bayesian fusion layer also helps — because it requires multiple engines to simultaneously indicate risk before the score rises to critical level. A single engine firing is not enough.

---

**Q4: Can you explain the Bayesian Network fusion? Why not a simple weighted average?**

> **A:** A weighted average treats the four engine scores as independent, but they're not. For example: if the Collusion Graph shows that two employees are co-accessing resources, that should make us more suspicious about the Access Void Score of those same employees. A simple average can't capture that conditional relationship. The Bayesian Network encodes these dependencies explicitly — it's a probabilistic graphical model where the conditional probability of the final DITS score given each engine's output is learned from training data. This gives us a calibrated probability estimate rather than an arbitrary number.

---

**Q5: Is this DPDP Act 2023 compliant? What data do you store?**

> **A:** Yes, fully compliant. Raw telemetry — keystrokes, screen activity, access logs — is processed in-stream and never persisted. Only the derived feature vectors, which are mathematical summaries that cannot be reverse-engineered back to individual actions, are stored. All ML inference runs on-premises within the bank's own servers, so no employee data leaves the bank's infrastructure. Identity is revealed only at Tier 3 intervention (score 70+), and that requires dual manager authorization — the four-eyes principle. We also auto-generate XBRL-compliant audit logs for RBI reporting.

---

**Q6: How does it integrate with existing Bank of Baroda systems without disrupting operations?**

> **A:** Integration is entirely non-invasive. We connect to Finacle and CBS via Apache Kafka webhooks — no modification to the core banking system is needed. IAM and LDAP connect via the SCIM 2.0 standard, which is an industry-standard protocol. PAM sessions feed in through the session recorder's API. The SOC dashboard integrates with existing SIEM tools like Splunk via REST webhooks. Deployment is on Kubernetes, so it scales independently of the bank's existing infrastructure. For 8,200 Bank of Baroda branches, we partition the Kafka topics by branch ID, giving linear horizontal scalability.

**Q8: What is your model's accuracy? ROC-AUC?**

> **A:** Our target ROC-AUC for the DITS fusion model is ≥0.93, with a false-positive rate below 0.3%. The Temporal Causal Chain model on CERT v6.2 achieves approximately 0.91 AUC in literature benchmarks with similar architectures. The Negative Access Profiler is novel so there is no direct benchmark — we validate it on our synthetic dataset with injected fraud scenarios. Full calibration results will be available in our Week 6 milestone.

---

**Q9: What happens if a fraudster knows about PHANTOM and tries to game it?**

> **A:** The system is designed to be adversarially robust in several ways. First, the Negative Access Profiler is hard to game — if you try to maintain your normal access pattern to avoid detection, you still have to do the fraud at some point, and the Temporal Chain will catch it. If you change your access pattern, the Avoidance Profiler catches it. Second, the Collusion Graph means that gaming the system alone is not enough — your co-conspirators also have to fool their own profiles simultaneously. Third, the Justification NLP catches the language drift that usually accompanies attempted cover-up. The multi-engine approach means an adversary has to beat all four simultaneously.

---

**Q10: Why specifically privileged access misuse and not account takeover or KYC fraud?**

> **A:** Account takeover and KYC fraud have well-established solutions — behavioral biometrics, liveness detection, device fingerprinting. The market is saturated with those. Privileged access misuse is the unsolved problem. India loses thousands of crores annually to employee-originated fraud and the detection gap is measured in months. Additionally, the problem statement specifically cites insider threats and privileged access misuse as key risk vectors. We chose depth over breadth — being the best in the world at one thing rather than mediocre at five.

---

**Q11: How does the multi-party collusion detection work specifically?**

> **A:** We build a bipartite graph — two types of nodes: employees and bank resources (accounts, modules, records). An edge is drawn between an employee and a resource when they access it. Then we add time-windowed co-access edges between pairs of employees who accessed the same resource within a configurable time window. The Heterogeneous Attention Network then learns to identify subgraphs where the pattern of connections indicates coordination — for example, Employee A accesses records 1–100, Employee B accesses records 101–200, and both touch the same report-generation module in overlapping sessions. Individually normal, collectively a signal. The model outputs a cluster-level risk score, not just individual scores.

---

**Q12: What is IndicBERT and why not just use GPT or a generic BERT?**

> **A:** IndicBERT is a multilingual BERT model pre-trained on 12 Indian languages including Hindi, Bengali, Tamil, and English. We chose it because Indian bank employees write internal notes in Hindi, Hinglish (mixed Hindi-English), and regional languages. A standard English BERT model would either fail to parse these or mistranslate them and produce unreliable scores. We fine-tune IndicBERT specifically on annotated banking justification texts — labeled as legitimate versus suspicious — to teach it the domain-specific language patterns of financial fraud concealment.

Additionally, the Negative Access Profiler specifically cannot be expressed as a rule — it requires statistical modeling of peer group behavior and dynamic threshold computation. Rules also produce enormous numbers of false positives because they can't reason about context the way our Bayesian fusion model does.

The real problem in this space is genuinely unsolved at a deep level, and the real-world stakes in Indian banking are enormous. The New India Co-operative Bank fraud (₹122 crore stolen by a General Manager using authorized access over five years), the IndusInd Bank internal fraud (₹1,960 crore in derivatives manipulated by legitimate employees), and the global Coinbase case (support agents bribed to use their valid credentials to exfiltrate customer data) all share one characteristic: the perpetrator never violated a single permission. They were authorized to do exactly what they did. Every existing security tool completely missed them.

The Core Insight: The Negative Access Signal
The breakthrough idea that makes PHANTOM genuinely novel is a reframing of the fundamental question. Every existing insider threat system asks: "Is what this employee doing unusual?" PHANTOM asks a completely different question that no existing product properly addresses: "What is this employee strategically choosing NOT to do — and why?"
This is the Negative Access Signal. In documented Indian banking fraud cases, forensic post-mortems consistently reveal that perpetrators — in the days and weeks before executing fraud — quietly stop touching audit-heavy functions, avoid systems that generate automated compliance alerts, stop filing manual overrides (which create paper trails), and route their access through paths that minimize logging. Their absence from certain functions is as loud as their presence in others. This behavioral contraction before a fraud event is the earliest detectable pre-crime signal in the entire insider threat kill chain, and not a single commercial UEBA tool models it because all anomaly detection is built exclusively on what users do, never on what they don't do.

The Solution: PHANTOM
PHANTOM stands for Privileged-access Holistic Anomaly and Multi-party Threat Operations Network. It is a four-engine AI system that detects insider fraud by modeling the temporal, linguistic, social, and strategic behavioral signatures of employees planning to misuse privileged bank access — triggering graduated interventions proportional to risk without disrupting legitimate operations.
Engine 1 — Temporal Causal Chain Detector: This engine models every privileged employee's access events as a heterogeneous temporal graph. Nodes represent employees, accounts, banking modules, customer records, transactions, and reports. Edges are typed and timestamped, encoding the semantic relationship between consecutive access events. A Graph Transformer (Graphformer architecture) is trained on the CERT Insider Threat Dataset v6.2, fine-tuned on synthetic Indian banking operation logs, to detect pre-crime access choreography — the specific sequence of individually authorized actions that together signal reconnaissance, privilege enumeration, and target identification — firing 2–5 days before any data exfiltration occurs.
Engine 2 — Negative Access Profiler: The most original contribution. For each employee, a Peer Cohort Access Distribution is built — the statistical distribution of which modules, records, and functions employees with the same role, branch, and tenure normally access across a rolling window. An Isolation Forest then computes the Access Void Score: how many items in the employee's expected access universe are they consistently not touching? The model distinguishes between normal voids (leave, seasonal workflow) and strategic voids (deliberate avoidance of audit-generating functions before a fraud event). No existing tool does this.
Engine 3 — Multi-Party Collusion Graph: The most complex engine, targeting the hardest unsolved problem in banking insider threat detection. A Heterogeneous Attention Network (HAN) models cross-identity co-access patterns across all employees. It builds a bipartite graph where employees and banking resources are two sets of nodes, and edges encode temporal co-access (when two employees accessed the same resource within a configurable time window). The model detects coordinated fraud rings — groups of employees who individually appear completely benign but collectively exhibit temporal co-access clustering, role-crossing resource bridges, and sparse but coordinated access patterns consistent with multi-party fraud. Every major documented Indian banking insider fraud has involved multiple colluding parties that individual anomaly detectors would have completely missed.
Engine 4 — Justification NLP Scorer: Every privileged action in a bank generates a text trail — override reason fields, incident ticket notes, supervisor approval comments, audit justification fields. These are completely ignored by every existing insider threat tool. PHANTOM fine-tunes IndicBERT (a multilingual transformer supporting Hindi, Hinglish, and English — essential for Indian PSBs where internal notes mix languages) on a labeled corpus of legitimate and suspicious justification texts. The model scores every justification across four dimensions: semantic vagueness, template reuse coefficient (cosine similarity to the employee's own past justifications), urgency injection markers, and social engineering language patterns. A corrupt employee justifying irregular access with "Customer urgently requested, senior approved, normal procedure" has a statistically distinct linguistic fingerprint from a legitimate override.

The DITS Score and Risk-Gated Intervention
The four engine outputs are fused using a Bayesian network — not a simple weighted average — because the engines are not statistically independent. A high Collusion Score should elevate the weight of the Negative Access Signal in the same employee cluster; Bayesian fusion encodes these conditional dependencies explicitly. The output is a Dynamic Identity Trust Score (DITS) from 0–100, updated every 30 seconds per employee during active sessions.
Interventions are strictly proportional to score to preserve the friction-optimized access the problem statement explicitly requires:

Score 0–39 (Trusted): Full access granted, passive logging only, zero user friction, silent baseline profiling continues
Score 40–69 (Elevated): Silent SOC alert, dual-approval gate for high-value operations, extended session recording triggered, re-authentication at next sensitive action
Score 70–100 (Critical): Instant session freeze, mandatory human supervisor review, automated RBI-ready evidence package generated, four-eyes principle governs identity reveal
